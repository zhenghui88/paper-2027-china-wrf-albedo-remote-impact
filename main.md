# Nonlocal atmospheric response to satellite-constrained soil albedo in Northwest China improves climate simulations of North China

# Abstract {-}

North China is vulnerable to late-spring to early-summer warm and dry conditions that threaten winter wheat production, yet the contribution of upstream soil albedo errors to simulating these events remains unexplored. Here we show that satellite-constrained soil albedo parameters over Northwest China identifiably improve Weather Research and Forecasting Model simulations of the 2020 warm and dry period in North China. The albedo correction increases effective soil albedo over arid Northwest China, reducing net shortwave absorption and sensible heating and inducing deep tropospheric cooling through boundary layer and cloud microphysical processes. This cooling shifts the upper-level westerly jet southward over Northwest to North China, altering the spatial pattern of upper-level divergence and convergence in a way that improves the simulation of surface temperature and precipitation. These results identify an overlooked upstream source of model drawback and demonstrate that constraining dryland soil albedo with satellite observations improves simulations of downstream temperature and precipitation.

# Introduction

North China is a major winter wheat production region vulnerable to late-spring to early-summer drought during critical growth stages [@zeng2026CEE]. Concurrent precipitation deficits and anomalous warmth can rapidly deplete soil moisture, impair crop development, and increase fire risk. Previous studies have attributed these droughts to large-scale drivers, including East Asian circulation variability and sea surface temperature anomalies in the Pacific and Indian Oceans [@zhang2015JC; @yang2005CJG], and have highlighted the importance of local land-atmosphere interactions in drought development and simulation [@zhang2024JGRAb]. Remote upstream land-surface forcing can also influence the climate of North China through anomalous atmospheric circulation [@ding2009IJC]. For example, sensitivity experiments have shown that perturbations in bulk surface albedo over the Tibetan Plateau affect the climate of North China [@lu2021JC; @sooraj2019CD; @smyth2021JC], and diagnostic analyses of reanalysis data have identified a statistical relationship between enhanced spring sensible heating in Northwest China and persistent summer drought in North China [@zhou2012CJAS; @huang2013CJAS; @huang2003CJAS].

Surface albedo is a fundamental land-surface parameter that controls the absorption of solar radiation and thus the energy available for turbulent and radiative exchange [@henderson-sellers1983RG; @dickinson1983AiG]. Previous numerical studies have examined its climate effects by applying idealized perturbations to bulk surface albedo [@lu2021JC; @sooraj2019CD; @smyth2021JC; @myhre2005GRL] or by directly replacing bulk albedo with satellite observations [@meng2018ERL; @myhre2005GRLa]. However, these approaches are not designed to resolve the detailed land-atmosphere interactions that arise when albedo is modified as a soil-specific parameter coupled to evolving model states. By modifying bulk albedo without distinguishing its physical source—such as snow, soil, or vegetation—they break the dynamic coupling between surface albedo and evolving model states, including soil moisture.

In arid and sparsely vegetated regions, soil albedo strongly regulates sensible heating and boundary layer development [@albertson1995WRR; @ma2011BM]. Northwest China is representative of such arid environments. To address the limitations of the bulk-albedo perturbation approaches described above, @ma2025SCES recently used satellite-constrained soil albedo parameters over the Tibetan Plateau and showed improved local model performance. Northwest China likewise lies upstream of North China along the midlatitude westerly jet and exhibits strong land-atmosphere coupling. However, it remains unclear whether observationally constrained soil albedo parameters over Northwest China can improve simulations of downstream climate in North China and, if so, through what mechanisms. We hypothesize that soil albedo changes in arid Northwest China can trigger deep tropospheric cooling or warming, which shifts the upper-level westerly jet via thermal wind adjustment and thereby alters downstream temperature and precipitation in North China. Understanding this nonlocal feedback is important for improving the simulation of regional climate extremes over the East Asian monsoon transition zone.

Here we investigate this question using the 2020 late-spring to early-summer period in North China as a test case. During this period, North China experienced recurrent drought in mid- to late April and again from mid-May to early June, with precipitation deficits coinciding with extreme heat: the earliest regional heatwave since 1961 occurred on May 1–9, and an intense heat event in early June pushed temperatures above 40°C locally, together impairing winter wheat growth [@DaiTanLong2021Qi]. We conduct paired experiments over China with the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model, one with default soil albedo parameters (CTL) and the other with MODIS (Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer)-constrained soil albedo parameters (SC). We show that the constrained soil albedo exerts its strongest effects over arid Northwest China, with a minor contribution from the Tibetan Plateau, triggering a nonlocal circulation response that improves the simulation of temperature and precipitation in North China. These results underscore the importance of constraining upstream dryland land-surface parameters for regional climate simulation during the pre-monsoon to early-monsoon transition in North China.

# Results

## Local Surface Forcing and Energy Budget

The satellite-constrained simulation increases both dry- (Fig. S1a) and saturated- (Fig. S1b) soil albedo predominantly over Northwest China, with a smaller contribution from the Tibetan Plateau (black outline in Fig. 1b). This albedo increase directly reduces net shortwave radiation at the surface (Fig. 1c). In response, the surface cools and emits less upward longwave radiation, so that net longwave radiation increases (Fig. 1d). Although this longwave increase partially offsets the shortwave deficit, net radiation still decreases. Because the forcing region is highly arid (Fig. S1c), the surface energy partition is heavily skewed toward sensible heating; the reduction in net radiation is therefore almost entirely balanced by a marked decrease in sensible heat flux (Fig. 1e), while latent heat flux remains largely unaffected due to limited moisture availability (Fig. 1f).

Although the albedo forcing is confined to Northwest China and the Tibetan Plateau (black outline in Fig. 1), discernible surface energy budget anomalies emerge downstream over North China (green box in Fig. 1). Because no soil parameters were altered in these downstream regions (Fig. S1a and S1b), the remote flux anomalies indicate that the upstream thermal forcing has triggered a large-scale atmospheric adjustment, motivating our investigation into the atmospheric teleconnection bridge.

## The Vertical Profile

To elucidate how the surface energy anomalies project downstream, we first examine the vertical thermal structure. A longitude-altitude cross-section averaged over 30–50°N reveals that the reduced sensible heat flux cools the entire tropospheric column over the forcing region (Fig. 2a). This temperature deficit extends from the surface up to the upper troposphere (\~200 hPa), with the most pronounced cooling concentrated in the mid-to-lower layers. Heat budget analysis averaged over this region indicates that the deep cooling is primarily driven by local diabatic processes rather than temperature advection, which remains weak (Fig. 2c).

Partitioning the diabatic heating reveals a distinct vertical structure, reflecting the different physical processes governing the heat budget. In the WRF model, total diabatic heating is decomposed into contributions from the planetary boundary layer (PBL) scheme, cloud microphysics, and radiation. Cooling in the lower troposphere (below 600 hPa) is dominated by planetary boundary layer (PBL) mixing. This term represents the vertical redistribution of heat driven by surface sensible heating and turbulent mixing. The reduced surface sensible heating suppresses the upward transport of turbulent kinetic energy, resulting in a measurably shallower PBL (Fig. S2) and a corresponding reduction in the vertical heat flux, which manifests as a cooling anomaly in the lower layers.

This contrasts with findings over the Southern Great Plains, where higher albedo induced lower-tropospheric warming via atmospheric radiative heating [@park2010JAS]; in the arid Northwest China, such radiative heating is weak, allowing turbulent cooling to dominate. Together, these results confirm that the surface anomaly is efficiently transported upward through turbulent and microphysical processes, constructing a vertical bridge to the large-scale circulation.

## Upper-level Circulation Adjustment

The deep cooling produces a negative geopotential height anomaly at 200 hPa over the forcing region, with a maximum reduction of approximately 20 gpm (Fig. 3a). This cold-core anomaly at high altitudes induces a localized cyclonic circulation adjustment around the cooling center. According to the thermal wind relationship, the altered meridional temperature gradient modulates the East Asian Subtropical Westerly Jet (EASWJ). Specifically, the localized thermal forcing leads to a southward shift of the jet axis, weakening its northern branch and strengthening its southern branch (Fig. 3a).

North China lies at the southern edge of the EASWJ, where the jet shift exerts a direct dynamical influence. The associated velocity potential and divergent wind anomalies indicate that the remote response is expressed mainly through upper-level circulation adjustment over North China (Fig. 3b), with the spatial pattern of upper-level divergence and convergence exerting a direct influence on mid-tropospheric vertical motion (Fig. 3c–d).

Furthermore, the stability of the low-level monsoon flow supports the conclusion that the response is a localized adjustment rather than a broad reorganization of the East Asian Summer Monsoon (EASM) system (Fig. 3d). This indicates that the albedo-induced forcing influences downstream climate through a specific nonlocal pathway centered on jet-stream adjustment, without requiring fundamental changes in the monsoon system\'s structure.

## Impacts on Downstream Climate

The surface temperature and precipitation response over North China exhibits physical consistency with the upper-level circulation adjustments described previously. Specifically, upper-level convergence corresponds to localized surface warming and precipitation reduction, whereas upper-level divergence aligns with surface cooling and precipitation enhancement (Fig. S3).

Evaluation against CMA station observations shows that the satellite-constrained (SC) simulation more accurately captures the spatial pattern and temporal evolution of temperature and precipitation anomalies during the study period, with reduced root-mean-square error (RMSE) and improved correlation. For surface air temperature, the SC simulation reduces the RMSE from 3.13°C to 2.99°C and increases the mean temporal correlation from 0.60 to 0.62. The spatial correlation across the observation sites for temperature improves from 0.72 to 0.73. For precipitation, the SC simulation mitigates the systemic dry bias, with the regional mean bias improving from −0.81 mm day⁻¹ to −0.04 mm day⁻¹, and the RMSE decreasing from 20.10 mm day⁻¹ to 19.76 mm day⁻¹ (Fig. 4). The spatial correlation for precipitation improves from 0.78 to 0.81. The improvements in downstream temperature simulations are statistically significant at the 95% confidence level (p<0.05).

The improvement is more pronounced during the later sub-period (June 15--July 10), when the jet typically resides over North China and is more directly influenced by upstream circulation changes (Fig. 5). During the earlier sub-period (May 10--June 15), the jet migrates northward and the improvement is less pronounced but still evident. These results demonstrate that satellite-constrained soil albedo over Northwest China improves the simulation of temperature and precipitation in North China through the nonlocal circulation adjustment described above.

# Discussion

The results presented in this study demonstrate that satellite-constrained soil albedo over the arid region of Northwest China improves the skill of WRF simulations for both temperature and precipitation in North China. This improvement is particularly evident during the late-spring to early-summer period (May to July), a critical transition season when the East Asian monsoon system is developing and the East Asian Subtropical Westerly Jet (EASWJ) resides directly over the North China region.

These findings highlight the importance of constraining upstream land-surface parameters, such as soil albedo, to improve the simulation of downstream weather extremes. This underscores the potential for satellite-based model parameter constraints to identify and mitigate sources of model error in remote areas that have notable consequences for regional climate simulation. However, limitations of this study should be recognized. Because this analysis focuses on a single year, the results should be interpreted as a process-based event study; future work with multi-year ensembles is needed to assess climatological generality.

# Methods

## Regional Model and Configuration

We use the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model version 4.3.2 [@skamarock2021] coupled with the Noah-MP land surface model [@niu2011JGRA]. The modeling domain covers China and adjacent regions at 3-km horizontal grid spacing (Fig. 1a), allowing deep convection to be resolved explicitly rather than parameterized. The model has 55 vertical levels extending to 50 hPa, with enhanced resolution in the planetary boundary layer to better capture near-surface thermal structure and vertical mixing.

The physical parameterization suite includes Thompson microphysics [@thompson2004MWR], the Rapid Radiative Transfer Model for General Circulation Models (RRTMG) longwave and shortwave radiation schemes [@iacono2008JGRA], and the Mellor–Yamada–Nakanishi–Niino planetary boundary layer scheme [@nakanishi2009JMSJ]. Initial and 3-hourly lateral boundary conditions are derived from the ERA5 reanalysis at 0.25° resolution [@hersbach2020QJRMS].

## Experimental Design

In Noah-MP, soil albedo is parameterized as a function of soil moisture and two parameters: dry-soil albedo and saturated-soil albedo. In the control experiment (CTL), these parameters are set to 0.235 and 0.135, respectively, and applied uniformly across the domain. In the sensitivity experiment (SC), they vary spatially among 20 soil albedo (soil color) classes, consistent with those used in the Community Land Model [@lawrence2007JGR]. The soil color class at each grid cell is identified by evaluating all 20 classes in offline Noah-MP simulations driven by the Global Land Data Assimilation System and selecting the class that best matches the bulk surface albedo observed by MODIS MCD43C3. Further details of the calibration are provided by @ChenJinYan2024C; these constrained parameters were previously used by @ma2025SCES and improved coupled land-atmosphere simulations over the Tibetan Plateau. In this study, we apply an additional filter that retains only grid cells where the constrained dry- or wet-soil albedo parameter increases by more than 0.04 relative to CTL, corresponding to the uncertainty of the MODIS albedo product; at all other grid cells, SC uses the same parameters as CTL. This filter isolates robust upstream forcing and focuses the analysis on its remote impact on North China.

Both CTL and SC are initialized on April 1, 2020, with April 1 to May 9 treated as spin-up. The analysis period spans May 10 to July 10, during which the East Asian Subtropical Westerly Jet typically resides over North China. Because each experiment consists of a single realization, the results should be interpreted as a process-based demonstration.

## Observations and Evaluation Metrics

Model performance was rigorously validated using multisource observations. Surface air temperature (2-m) was evaluated against daily observations from the China Meteorological Administration (CMA) station network. Precipitation was assessed using the Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) Integrated Multi-satellitE Retrievals (IMERG) product.

To ensure a consistent grid-to-grid comparison for precipitation, the 3-km WRF output was regridded to the 0.1° GPM IMERG grid using a conservative remapping method. Evaluation metrics include the mean bias, temporal and spatial correlation coefficients (R), and root-mean-square error (RMSE). Regional averages and evaluation metrics were calculated over the North China target region (112–125°E, 31–39°N) to quantify the downstream impacts of the upstream albedo forcing.
