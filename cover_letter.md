# Cover Letter for Manuscript Submission

Subject: Submission of original research article for consideration

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Nonlocal atmospheric response to satellite-constrained soil albedo in Northwest China improves North China simulations", for consideration as a Research Article in *npj Climate and Atmospheric Science*.

North China is a major winter wheat production region that is vulnerable to late-spring to early-summer warm and dry conditions. Simulating these events remains challenging because model errors may originate not only within the affected region but also in dynamically connected upstream areas. In this study, we investigate whether soil albedo errors over arid Northwest China can influence downstream temperature and precipitation simulations in North China through a nonlocal atmospheric pathway.

Using paired 3-km WRF simulations for the 2020 late-spring to early-summer warm and dry period, we show that MODIS-constrained soil albedo parameters increase effective soil albedo primarily over arid Northwest China. This reduces net shortwave absorption and surface sensible heating, induces deep tropospheric cooling through boundary-layer and cloud microphysical processes, and shifts the upper-level westerly jet southward via thermal wind adjustment. The resulting circulation response alters upper-level divergence and mid-tropospheric vertical motion over North China, improving the simulated spatial pattern and temporal evolution of surface temperature and precipitation.

The main contributions of this manuscript are:

- It identifies upstream dryland soil albedo as an overlooked source of regional model uncertainty for downstream temperature and precipitation simulations.
- It demonstrates a physically coherent nonlocal pathway linking Northwest China land-surface forcing to North China atmospheric conditions through tropospheric thermal adjustment and westerly-jet modulation.
- It shows that satellite-constrained land-surface parameters can improve regional climate simulations beyond the local forcing region.

We believe the manuscript fits well within the scope of *npj Climate and Atmospheric Science*, particularly its interest in atmospheric dynamics, weather and climate extremes, climate prediction and simulation, and atmosphere-land interactions. The study provides process-based evidence that improving land-surface parameter constraints in arid upstream regions can help reduce downstream simulation errors in a region of major agricultural importance.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. Data availability and open research information are provided in the manuscript.
